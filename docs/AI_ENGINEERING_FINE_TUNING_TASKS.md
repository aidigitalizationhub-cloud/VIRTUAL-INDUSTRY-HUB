# Tasks for the Fine-Tuned AI on the Platform

## Purpose

The fine-tuned AI will assist researchers, administrators, and TTO/IP officers throughout the research-disclosure and technology-transfer process.

It will provide screening, extraction, evidence organization, and recommendations. It will not make legal decisions or replace a human reviewer.

## Platform Tasks

## Information Sources the AI Uses

The AI must extract information from the following platform sources:

- Project title, summary, description, research area, intended use, and project status.
- Completed IP disclosure questions and researcher declarations.
- Researcher, inventor, contributor, institution, department, sponsor, grant, and funding records.
- Dates for creation, submission, publication, presentation, demonstration, or other public disclosure.
- Uploaded technical briefs, papers, supporting documents, requested evidence, and approved private files.
- Citations, URLs, external links, and references submitted by the researcher.
- Platform metadata such as owner, timestamps, workflow status, review route, and audit history.
- Administrative and TTO/IP findings, reviewer questions, and responses already recorded in the case.
- Permitted external sources such as institutional repositories, publications, patent databases, and approved research indexes.

The AI must preserve the source of every extracted fact and finding. It must distinguish between:

- A statement made by the researcher.
- Evidence contained in an uploaded document.
- Metadata generated by the platform.
- A human reviewer’s finding.
- Information found in a permitted external source.

The AI must not treat general model knowledge, unlinked text, or an unavailable source as evidence. When a source is missing, inaccessible, contradictory, or not permitted, the result must identify the gap and recommend human review.

### External Sources for Reports and Findings

The fine-tuned AI will receive bounded evidence from these external sources:

| Source | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Researcher-provided links | Check the researcher’s publications, patents, reports, and cited references. |
| OpenAlex | Search related scholarly works, authors, institutions, and publication records. |
| Crossref | Verify publication metadata, DOI records, titles, and dates. |
| University news records | Check public institutional announcements and possible prior disclosures. |
| Google Patents | Search public patent records and similar technical concepts. |
| WIPO PATENTSCOPE | Search international patent publications and related records. |
| WIPO IP portal | Provide an institutional IP search reference for authorized reviewers. |

The AI must use these sources to write evidence-linked reports and findings. Every external claim must cite the supplied source ID and URL. Search results are leads until a reviewer confirms the underlying record. The AI must report when a source is unavailable and must never invent a citation or treat a search result as proof of patentability, novelty, ownership, infringement, or legal safety.

### 1. Understand Submitted Research

The AI will read project descriptions, disclosure answers, uploaded documents, and permitted references to identify:

- What the technology or research is.
- Which problem it addresses.
- How the proposed solution works.
- The claimed benefits and intended applications.
- The people, institutions, sponsors, and partners involved.

### 2. Extract Important Facts

The AI will extract and organize:

- Inventors and contributors.
- Funding and grant information.
- Sponsorship and collaboration details.
- Important dates.
- Publications, demonstrations, presentations, and public disclosures.
- Technical claims.
- Citations, links, and supporting documents.

### 3. Identify IP and Confidentiality Risks

The AI will flag information that may require human review, including:

- Possible patent-related subject matter.
- Confidential or sensitive technical information.
- Previous public disclosure that may affect protection options.
- Unclear ownership or inventorship.
- Sponsor, employment, grant, or institutional obligations.
- Missing contributor, funding, or disclosure information.
- Content that should not be published automatically.

### 4. Check Evidence and Consistency

The AI will compare the project description, disclosure answers, documents, and permitted sources to identify:

- Unsupported claims.
- Conflicting names, dates, or facts.
- Missing evidence.
- Evidence that does not support a claim.
- Repeated, copied, generic, or inconsistent content.

The AI will explain what needs to be verified instead of declaring a project authentic or fraudulent.

### 5. Find Similar Research and Prior Art

When approved reference sources are available, the AI will:

- Extract technical keywords and concepts.
- Find potentially similar research, publications, or patent records.
- Explain why a source appears relevant.
- Link each result to its source.
- Separate strong matches, weak matches, and unrelated results.

The AI will not decide whether an invention is novel, patentable, or infringing.

### 6. Recommend the Review Route

Based on the available evidence, the AI will recommend whether a submission should receive:

- Normal processing.
- Administrative review.
- TTO/IP review.
- Additional evidence collection.
- Human review because the result is uncertain or incomplete.

The recommendation will never approve, reject, or publish a submission automatically.

### 7. Prepare Reviewer Summaries

The AI will create a short review brief containing:

- A plain-language project summary.
- Key extracted facts.
- Detected risks.
- Supporting evidence.
- Missing or conflicting information.
- Recommended next action.
- Confidence and limitations.

### 8. Assist with Public and Private Content Separation

The AI will help distinguish:

- Information suitable for a public project summary.
- Information that should remain private.
- Technical details that require TTO/IP clearance.
- Evidence that may only be shared with approved reviewers or partners.

Human reviewers remain responsible for the final publication and access decision.

## AI Boundaries

The AI must not claim that:

- An invention is patentable.
- A person or institution owns the IP.
- A patent will be granted.
- A disclosure is legally safe.
- A project is approved for publication.
- A project is authentic or fraudulent without human investigation.

The AI must clearly label its output as advisory. Uncertain, unsupported, malformed, or failed results must be sent to human review.

## Expected Result

The fine-tuned AI should make each submission easier to understand and review by turning unstructured research information into reliable facts, evidence-backed risk signals, similar-source references, and clear next-step recommendations.
